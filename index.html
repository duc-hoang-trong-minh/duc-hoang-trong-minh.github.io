About

My interest in research started early because my father is a professor. My first involvement in research was modest: I supported several projects, helped with technical tasks, and learned from senior students and supervisors. These early roles taught me how to enter an unfamiliar problem, understand its technical context, and contribute step by step.

My formal research foundation was built during my engineering studies in Electronics and Telecommunications at Hanoi University of Science and Technology. There, I developed a rigorous background in communication theory, signal processing, wireless systems, and physical-layer design. At INRS, I have expanded this foundation toward AI-enabled and security-aware communication systems, including semantic communication, adversarial learning, and O-RAN-based RF sensing.

Research interests

Semantic communications

Digital joint source-channel coding, semantic representation learning, adaptive transmission, and robustness of neural communication pipelines.

Wireless security

Physical-layer security, adversarial and backdoor attacks, secure adaptation, and trustworthy learning-enabled communication systems.

Physical-layer communications

Adaptive modulation, precoding, probabilistic constellation shaping, reinforcement learning, and performance analysis.

O-RAN and RF sensing

Real radio experimentation using uplink reference-signal channel estimates for device-free sensing and privacy-preserving situational awareness.
